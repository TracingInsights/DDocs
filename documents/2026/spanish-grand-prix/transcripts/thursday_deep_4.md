PART ONE – Fernando ALONSO (Aston Martin), Esteban OCON (Haas), Franco COLAPINTO (Alpine)

**Q:** Fernando, can we start with you? A second race on home soil for you and a return to Madrid for F1 in 45 years. Just how excited are you about this first visit of the Spanish Grand Prix to the Madring?

Fernando ALONSO:

Yeah, it’s going to be a very nice weekend. As you said, we have this privilege to race two times in Spain this year and, yeah, for the very first time in Madrid. I think last time was in Jarama, you know, many, many years ago. So, yeah, it is special. Also a new track, so it adds this challenge, you know, for everyone to learn the track as quick as possible in free practice. And, yeah, these cars as well, with the new regulations, always tricky to fine-tune energy here and there, and especially if it is a new track. So, yeah, a lot of challenges ahead and, for me, yeah, double special because being in Spain.

**Q:** Tell us a little bit more about the layout of the track, what you think of it. Have you driven it on the simulator yet?

**FA:**

Yeah, I drove in the simulator, but maybe it’s a better question for the Ferrari drivers.

**Q:** They were here earlier in the year, of course, on a filming day.

What about car performance then, Fernando? Do you expect to be more competitive here than you were at Monza last weekend?

**FA:**

Expect to be more competitive than Monza, in any other circuit in the world, probably. But let’s wait and see. I think, as I said, there are a couple of challenges to discover. I think there is a lot of kerb riding, as we saw in the simulator and on the layout, and the confirmation from Formula 3 as well that it was, yeah, not an easy track. And then, yeah, the grip level on the asphalt is a question mark. You know, is it going to be grippy or not? Track evo, you know, from FP1 to qualifying and race. Normally, in a street circuit, you know, there is a huge improvement, so you need to keep up with the pace. Every time you jump in the car, normally the track will be faster than your previous run and, yeah, you need to anticipate that from your driver input, but also in the set-up directions. So, yes, there are interesting things that will happen during the weekend and, yeah, looking forward, especially accumulating laps. I think in every street circuit you need to do as many laps as possible to get that confidence close to the walls and extract the lap time. And at the same time, you have yellow flags, red flags, all these different things happening and sometimes you are lucky, unlucky, if you are in the box or you are out on track. So, we hope for a good weekend, no issues on the car and good reliability, and accumulating laps. That will be the key.

**Q:** Final one from me. When can we expect to hear news about your plans for the future, for 2027?

**FA:**

Not soon.

**Q:** Not soon? Okay. Why the delay?

**FA:**

Because I think it’s nice, no?

**Q:** Fernando, thanks very much. Esteban, let’s come to you now. Can we start with your thoughts on Madrid and racing here?

Esteban OCON:

I think it’s awesome. Awesome to be here in Madrid and awesome to be learning a new track. I think it’s been a while since I came to F1 where I’m learning a new circuit, coming to a new place. Obviously, it’s a crazy circuit. There’s a lot of risk-reward on when to go fast, getting close to the walls. Yes, that’s going to reward the people that take the most risk. But, yes, as Fernando said, there’s a lot of unknowns and that’s very good because hopefully it will bring opportunities for the cars in the midfield. The unknowns in terms of strategy or red flag in the race or whatever is going to happen, hopefully we can take those.

**Q:** You’ve had a few days to reflect on the chassis upgrades that the team brought to Monza. Where was the car better?

**EO:**

I think that it was positive. Yes, definitely, it did deliver for the whole team to have these updates. It was not the best track, of course, to test them in Monza, but it was very good that we got them there. We are still working with the inconsistency across cars. That’s what’s hurting us at the moment. Yes, I mean, this is what we need to try and solve this weekend. But the upgrade has been very positive and we are looking forward to seeing what kind of gain it gives us here.

**Q:** Okay, thank you for that. And Franco, let’s come to you now. You used to live in Madrid, so this is about as close to a home race as you’re going to get this season.

Franco COLAPINTO:

Yes, this season hopefully only. No, really excited to be back here in Spain, in Madrid. The second race of the year in Spain, which is very exciting. Madrid does feel like the closest to home, I think, especially because of all my, you know, previous experiences and how I started in Europe and how I started in Formula racing. I started here in Madrid, so to be racing here in Formula 1, it’s a dream come true. I think it’s very good for the sport as well, you know, to have another big city like Madrid being back on the calendar and, you know, putting a very exciting track. It looks like a very good one to drive as well. It’s going to be cool. So, yeah, looking forward to tomorrow and to the weekend.

**Q:** Now, Monza was a great weekend for the team. Do you think it’ll be a slightly tougher assignment here in Madrid?

**FC:**

Hopefully not, but I think it’s going to be a bit more difficult. Monza suited really well our car and the places where we were strong, especially straights, but also the low-speed corners, where we were quite good there. And then I think the upgrade did bring us a lot. That upgrade in Zandvoort was very positive for the car. It put the car in a much better window and, again, we could deliver a much better performance in Monza. So, I think still a lot of work to do, but hopefully we can translate it into a good weekend here in Madrid as well and have a quick car.

Well, I think, yeah, as we all said, I think the track seems difficult, first of all, to learn, very technical, a lot of kerb riding and a couple of high-speed sections where you need to get very close to the walls, which is very attractive and very high adrenaline for a driver. If that’s the case, I think we have a very good weekend in front of us as a show, you know, for everyone else.
