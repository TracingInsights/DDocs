**Q:** (Jon Noble – The Race) To James and Flavio. It seems the top four teams have made a step away from the pack over the course of this regulation set and this season. What prospect do you see of anyone breaking that stranglehold over the next few years, and how much harder is that job made with the cost cap?

**JV:**

It’s a really good question. First and foremost, I own the mistakes that we made this winter, and we need to detach that from that question. So we have gone backwards. We had a car that was overweight. But actually, when we did the cost cap, this is way back in COVID, when we had a phone call about it, there were three key tasks that we wanted out of it. We wanted long-term financial sustainability, and I think in that goal, not just have we achieved it, but we’ve exceeded it. This business now has good investment that allows us to go on, and they’re businesses. They weren’t businesses before 2021 at all. The two others, though, that went with it were sporting equity and, effectively, compression of the field, so effectively competitiveness that compressed together. Those are the three goals of cost cap, and those two have failed. There’s a bit of statistics I was looking at about a month ago, which is the following: if I take the last 15 years of the sport, the five before, sorry, the five that we’ve been in as cost cap right now, currently the top four teams score 83% of the points. Before that it was in the 70s, just to be clear about it. In other words, we have made the situation worse, where the top four are the top four and all the rest of us are fighting over, ballpark, 17% of the points. Number of times on the podium used to be 10% of the time, now it’s 5% of the time. I can give you a whole bunch of statistics, but the cost cap has made it worse, not better. I’m probably unique because – it’s not a compliment – I went from the best team to the worst team. I think that’s fairly clear. What I hadn’t appreciated or realised when you do that jump is just how little exists. When you’re in a cost cap, I’m not spending money on the car. I’m spending millions, tens of millions actually, just on basic systems, so you just know where a part is in the factory. So that’s not going to the car, it’s not going to a front wing, it’s not going to an upgrade. It’s going on systems that I need to do just to do the very basics. So, we’re caught in a cost cap trap, because if I do anything short-term, and I always talk about investing in the future, now you understand why. If I do anything that’s about this year, I will slow myself down in the future, and I can’t do that. We have a two-tier championship. That’s not me telling you that. That is just what is on the table right now in terms of points score. And I think, and this is where we have to come together as a business, that’s not right in terms of what we want for the future. We do want the ability for, if we do a good job, we should have the ability to fight for that. Even last year, which was a great year, the gap on points between us and P4, between us and Ferrari, was 238 points. It’s not a small amount. It’s an absolute gulf, and we were on the podium three times. In cost cap, and I didn’t appreciate it until I came into Williams, it really penalises you in terms of what you’re doing. The solution isn’t to take cost cap away. As I said before, we have a brilliantly financially stable sport now as a result of it, but we do need to think about what mechanisms we have in there to make sure we’re all playing with the same tools and the same mechanisms, because today we’re not. That’s a bit lengthy, but that’s my thoughts on it.
