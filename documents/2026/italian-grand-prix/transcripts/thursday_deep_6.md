**Q:** Lewis, please can we start with you. Home race for Ferrari. Just tell us how special it feels to be a Ferrari driver at Monza?

Lewis HAMILTON:

Good afternoon, everyone. Yeah. I mean, it’s the best feeling to be a Ferrari driver. It’s such an honour and a privilege to arrive in Italy at this iconic race with the most iconic and best team in the world. It’s really phenomenal to experience it for the second year in a row, and it still feels new. So, it’s not something you can get used to.

**Q:** One thing that is new this weekend is the Schumacher-inspired livery. Can we get your thoughts on that and what Michael meant to you?

**LH:**

I mean, I haven’t actually seen the livery, but I’m wearing a shirt with seven stars, which is quite fitting because I have seven stars too. So, it feels alright. It feels like it’s also mine. But, I mean, it’s kind of crazy. Last year, we celebrated Niki, who was a dear friend, and Michael, for me, I grew up… I remember the happiest Sunday mornings when you heard the music, the old, better music that they used to use for the start of Formula One. Was it ‘The Chain’? Yeah. So much better. And you could hear it downstairs starting and you knew, “Okay,” you get all excited. You run downstairs and I just always remember seeing Michael in the red car. And I remember playing computer games and always being Michael in that Ferrari and always just trying to imagine what it would feel like to be in his shoes. And I have the privilege of having that experience now. But, I mean, he was such a leader. Obviously, did such an amazing job throughout his career. And again, I had the privilege of racing in a moment where he was still racing and being on track with him, which meant a lot.

**Q:** In terms of performance, the power unit is getting an upgrade here. Will it be enough to fight for victory?

**LH:**

Every little helps. And I think, you know, up until this point of the year, we’ve been losing, even in the last race on such a short circuit, we were losing four tenths a lap through the race. So that’s a huge deficit and we’ve carried that through the year to this point. And what I can say is I’m just really, really proud of when I go back to the factory and I see how hard everyone is working, that they’re really just heads down. Everyone’s so enthusiastic. I see a different focus this year to last year. Last year, I felt like there wasn’t really a North Star. We were kind of, I think, doing the best we could, but not really knowing where exactly we’re trying to aim for. And now we have a North Star. We know we need to work towards. And I think they’ve done a tremendous job to really pull together and deliver. And this is a step forward. It’s not the whole gap that we need, but we knew that would be the case. But to see bits coming each weekend, adding to the car, it’s exciting to see that we are pushing and, you know, strongly still believe that we’ve got what it takes to win.

But, you know, nonetheless, it’s been a long time coming. The team have worked incredibly hard and, you know, we have a lot of challenges that we face. Being a small team, one of them is the turnaround times of our upgrades, and you’ve seen that. You know, the last big one that we brought was in Canada, which is a long way back. So, yeah, hopefully it’s going to be a good weekend for us.

And that’s what Ayao has been working incredibly hard to do. I’m sure we’ll get there. And, you know, this upgrade is a big step for us. And hopefully, like you said, it can reward us with some points. That will be the goal.

**QUESTIONS FROM THE FLOOR**

**Q:** (David Croft – Sky Sports F1) A question for you, Lewis. You touched on the celebration of Michael Schumacher’s time with Ferrari. What inspiration have you taken from what Michael did with the Scuderia? And how have you used that to give your input to the team? **LH:**

Well, I mean, I haven’t taken a page out of Michael’s book in that sense. I think, obviously, I wasn’t here in the team when, obviously, there was a big narrative of Michael turning the team around. But ultimately, it’s a combination of many, many people. And there’s no “I” in team. I think it’s like a huge ship and it’s the driver’s job to work alongside the head figures to try and steer it in the right direction. I think the positive thing, the opportunistic thing for a new driver coming in with experience, who has experienced two different teams, when you come from outside, when you’re in it, it’s sometimes hard to see the wood for the trees, you know. And it’s when someone comes in, even if new engineers come in from different places, straight away they say, “Oh, we did this differently,” and not necessarily that’s a better way, but it’s just sometimes different approaches. It’s quite phenomenal, really, if you think that all these teams have the same rules. They do everything differently to get to the same sort of… and then the cars on track are within a tenth. You know, it’s quite phenomenal, and that’s really what makes our sport so great. I think, for me, I pushed for a lot of changes within the car, which last year we couldn’t do, one, because of [Cost] Cap, two, because it’s what they’ve done all the time and it’s like, “Why should we change it?” So, it took a lot of persuading to get some of those changes done. And then some of them were expensive and they took a longer process. So, I worked in the sim and requested that they design it for the sim, and I tested in the sim last year, and then they added it as options for the car that we use every weekend. And, yeah, so just kind of the whole goal was, coming from an outside perspective, looking at all the things they do well — and nobody does everything perfect — highlighting the things that are maybe good or not as good and saying, “Hey, we need to level up in all these areas.” And that’s what I try to do. I think Fred even said there were some things on the list that he just can’t do because it’s too expensive. **Q:** (Mara Sangiorgio – Sky Sports Italy) A question to Lewis. Lewis, it’s right to say that you arrived in style this morning. Why and when did you buy your amazing F40? And what does it mean to you? Did you realise one of your red dreams?

**LH:**

100%. Yeah. It’s a dream car of mine. I’ve been looking for one for a while and I wanted one with low mileage. I wanted one with a story, and lots have come up in auction over the past couple of years, some with low mileage, but just not particularly special, or maybe the paint’s been redone or something like that. I wanted to find the most authentic one and managed to come across this one that the owner had bought it, drove it from Maranello to his home, 69 kilometres, and then parked it and never started it again. The passenger door had never been opened, you know. It just had this cover on it and all this dust. Then the decision was kind of, “What do you do? Do you keep it as an art piece, keep it all dusty, or do you have them clean it up?” So that took me a while to decide, and then I had them redo it because I wanted to drive it. Because I realised, actually, what’s kind of crazy is, I might be wrong, but I’m pretty sure none of the Ferrari drivers in history have ever arrived in the coolest cars that they have, you know, these iconic ones, the GTO or the F40. And I was like, “It has to be done.” What was – I know it’s a long answer – but what was also crazy is when they were taking the car apart to check it, firstly, it stood the test of time. Everything was still working. Hardly anything had to be changed. And when they took the engine out, they found out the engine’s number 44. It’s got stamped on the engine number 44. I couldn’t believe it. I was like, “Did you know? You’ve got to be lying.” So, it feels very much like fate. And I thought it was a real opportunity to kind of highlight the history of Ferrari in their home country, at their race, and when am I ever going to get to do that again?
